Now it's your turn to write the code.

There's a long tradition behind this first exercise: for decades, people learning a new programming language have started by making it print *Hello, world!*

One thing to know before you start: the checks compare your output **exactly**. `Hello, world!` and `hello world` look alike to a person, but to a computer they are different texts. Capital letters, commas and spaces all count.

A program can have more than one line. Python runs them in order, from top to bottom, and each `print()` starts a new line on the screen. You'll need that for the second exercise.
